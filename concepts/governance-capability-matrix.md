# Governance Capability Matrix

Use this matrix to assess which capabilities are needed for a domain, dataset, or data product.

| Capability | Basic | Managed | Advanced |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ownership | Owner named | Owner and steward actively manage decisions | Ownership tied to performance and funding routines |
| Glossary | Key terms listed | Definitions approved and versioned | Definitions linked to metrics, reports, and data products |
| Catalog | Dataset registered | Metadata, classification, and usage context maintained | Automated metadata updates and adoption analytics |
| Quality | Issues handled reactively | Rules, thresholds, and owners documented | Automated monitoring with business impact tracking |
| Lineage | Source noted | Major transformations and consumers mapped | End-to-end lineage integrated with change management |
| Access | Manual approvals | Role-based approvals and periodic review | Policy-driven access with automated evidence |
| Issue management | Ad hoc escalation | Triage workflow with severity and SLAs | Root-cause analytics and preventive controls |
| Compliance | Policy awareness | Controls mapped to obligations | Continuous evidence and audit-ready reporting |

## How to apply the matrix

1. Identify the business criticality and risk level of the data.
2. Select the target maturity level for each capability.
3. Prioritize gaps that block trust, compliance, reuse, or AI readiness.
4. Review progress quarterly with domain owners and governance forums.
