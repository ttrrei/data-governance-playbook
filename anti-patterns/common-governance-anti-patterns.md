# Common Governance Anti-Patterns

## Catalog everything before governing anything

Cataloging every asset without prioritization creates activity but little trust. Start with critical data, decisions, risks, and reusable products.

## Treating governance as a tool rollout

Tools help automate workflows and metadata, but they cannot replace ownership, decision rights, standards, and operating routines.

## Naming owners without authority

Owners who cannot make decisions or allocate remediation effort become symbolic. Assign ownership with mandate, capacity, and escalation support.

## Centralizing every decision

A central team can set standards and facilitate conflicts, but domains should own business meaning and day-to-day decisions.

## Measuring documentation instead of outcomes

Counting catalog entries or glossary terms is not enough. Track trust, reuse, issue resolution, access cycle time, quality trends, and risk reduction.

## Ignoring consumers

Governance designed only by policy teams may miss real usability problems. Include analysts, product teams, engineers, and operational users in feedback loops.

## Creating permanent exceptions

Exceptions should have owners, rationale, compensating controls, expiration dates, and review routines. Otherwise they become unmanaged policy drift.
